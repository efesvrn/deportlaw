Restriction codes and entry bans in Turkiye

A restriction code (tahdit kodu) is a letter and a number on your record at the Presidency of Migration Management. It tells border officers why you may not enter Turkiye, or on what conditions you may. Ç codes are mostly time-limited entry bans, G codes are security and health restrictions with no fixed end, N codes require prior permission, O codes concern international protection and V codes are notes on residence and visa status.

Each code below has its own page: what it means, the period usually applied, the legal basis and the realistic routes for having it lifted. By law an entry ban cannot exceed 5 years, but where there is a serious threat to public order or security it can be extended by up to 10 more years.

Overstay codes

Entered when you leave after staying past your visa, visa-free period or residence permit. The length of the ban follows the length of the overstay.

  • Ç-101Overstay Entry Ban (3 months)3 months
  • Ç-102Overstay Entry Ban (6 months)6 months
  • Ç-103Overstay Entry Ban (1 year)1 year
  • Ç-104Overstay Entry Ban (2 years)2 years
  • Ç-105Overstay Entry Ban (5 years)5 years

Work codes

These follow inspections under the International Labour Force Law (No. 6735). The worker usually faces deportation and a ban; the employer is fined separately.

  • Ç-117Working Without a Work Permit1 year

Debt codes

They block entry until a fine or cost owed to the state is paid. Paying it, and proving that you paid, is usually the first step.

  • Ç-119Unpaid Fine for Working Without a PermitUntil the debt is paid
  • Ç-120Unpaid Visa or Residence Overstay FineUntil the debt is paid
  • Ç-135Unpaid Fine Under Law No. 6458Until the debt is paid
  • Ç-136Unpaid Deportation Travel CostsUntil the debt is paid

Criminal-proceedings codes

These follow a criminal investigation, prosecution or conviction. What happens in the criminal file often decides what happens to the code.

  • Ç-113Illegal Entry or ExitUp to 5 years; the decision states the period
  • Ç-114Subject to Criminal ProceedingsUp to 5 years; the decision states the period
  • Ç-115Released From PrisonUp to 5 years; the decision states the period
  • Ç-116Public Order, Morality or Illegitimate IncomeUp to 5 years; the decision states the period
  • Ç-150Forged or Someone Else's Documents5 years
  • Ç-151Migrant Smuggling5 years

Border and departure codes

Entered when someone is refused entry or does not leave after being told to. They are often recorded together with another code.

  • Ç-137Did Not Leave After Being Invited ToUp to 5 years; the decision states the period
  • Ç-138Inadmissible Passenger (INAD)Up to 5 years; the decision states the period

Public-health codes

Tied to a medical condition that threatens public health. They can normally be removed with an up-to-date medical report.

  • Ç-118Public Health RiskUntil the health risk no longer exists
  • G-78Contagious DiseaseUntil the health risk no longer exists

Security codes

Entered on the basis of intelligence or security assessments. The file is usually classified, so the reasons often come out only in a lawsuit.

  • Ç-141International Security Risk5 years
  • Ç-149Suspected Foreign Terrorist Fighter5 years
  • Ç-152Precautionary Entry Ban1 year
  • G-82Activities Against National SecurityNo fixed end; lifted only by the administration or a court
  • G-87General Security ThreatNo fixed end; lifted only by the administration or a court
  • G-89Foreign Terrorist FighterNo fixed end; lifted only by the administration or a court

Prior-permission codes (N)

Not bans as such: entry requires prior permission (ön izin). In practice a refused permission works like a ban with no fixed end.

  • N-82Entry Subject to Prior PermissionEntry only with prior permission
  • N-99Interpol Red NoticeEntry only with prior permission

Protection codes

These concern international or temporary protection. They change what status you can hold and how you can return, more than they set a fixed ban.

  • O-100International Protection Application WithdrawnA note on your file, not a fixed ban on its own
  • V-87Temporary Protection Holder Who ReturnedReturn only with a visa or permission

Residence and visa notes (V)

Notes on your file. They do not always bar entry, but they shape every later residence application and border check.

  • V-69Residence Permit CancelledA note on your file, not a fixed ban on its own
  • V-70Marriage of ConvenienceA note on your file, not a fixed ban on its own
  • V-71False Address DeclarationA note on your file, not a fixed ban on its own
  • V-84Conditional Entry: Apply for Residence Within 10 DaysA note on your file, not a fixed ban on its own

How to find out which code you have

The code is printed in the entry ban section of the notification form given to you at the border or at the removal centre. If you were only handed an INAD (inadmissible passenger) form, it usually cites Law No. 6458 and nothing more: 6458 is the law, not your code.

In that case the code and the decision behind it can be requested from the Presidency of Migration Management. From abroad this is done through a lawyer in Turkiye who holds your power of attorney. Do not pay anyone online who offers to check your code for you.

Deadlines and legal routes

An entry ban is an administrative decision and can be challenged before the administrative court within 60 days of notification. Once those 60 days have passed, the realistic routes are an application to the administration to lift or shorten the ban and, where there is a serious reason such as family, health or education, a special annotated visa (özel meşruhatlı vize) requested through a Turkish consulate. That visa lets you enter while the code stays on record.

A deportation decision runs on a much shorter clock: 7 days from notification (Law No. 6458, Art. 53). Administrative fines are challenged separately, within 15 days, before the criminal judgeship of peace.
