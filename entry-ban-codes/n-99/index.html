Restriction code · N-99

N-99: Interpol Red Notice

N-99 is entered for a foreigner who is the subject of an Interpol red notice or arrest request. Entry depends on prior permission, and arriving at the border can lead to detention.

Code
N-99
Also written
N99
Category
Prior-permission codes (N)
Usual period
Entry only with prior permission
Legal basis
Law No. 6458 (Foreigners and International Protection), Art. 9

What N-99 means for you

N-99 has two fronts. In Turkiye the code and any extradition request go through the courts and the Ministry of Justice. At Interpol, deletion of the notice can be requested from the Commission for the Control of INTERPOL's Files. If the notice is deleted, that decision is the basis for removing N-99.

Prior-permission codes (N). Not bans as such: entry requires prior permission (ön izin). In practice a refused permission works like a ban with no fixed end.

Restriction codes are defined in internal instructions of the Presidency of Migration Management (the circular in use is numbered 2024/5), not in the law itself, and descriptions and periods differ between sources. The decision served on you is what counts.

How to find out which code you have

The code is printed in the entry ban section of the notification form given to you at the border or at the removal centre. If you were only handed an INAD (inadmissible passenger) form, it usually cites Law No. 6458 and nothing more: 6458 is the law, not your code.

In that case the code and the decision behind it can be requested from the Presidency of Migration Management. From abroad this is done through a lawyer in Turkiye who holds your power of attorney. Do not pay anyone online who offers to check your code for you.

Deadlines and legal routes

An entry ban is an administrative decision and can be challenged before the administrative court within 60 days of notification. Once those 60 days have passed, the realistic routes are an application to the administration to lift or shorten the ban and, where there is a serious reason such as family, health or education, a special annotated visa (özel meşruhatlı vize) requested through a Turkish consulate. That visa lets you enter while the code stays on record.

A deportation decision runs on a much shorter clock: 7 days from notification (Law No. 6458, Art. 53). Administrative fines are challenged separately, within 15 days, before the criminal judgeship of peace.

What to send us

A photo of every page of the decision or INAD form, the photo page of your passport, the date and border gate where you were stopped, and any residence permit, marriage or birth certificate that ties you to Turkiye. If you have paid a fine, the receipt too. With these we can tell you which route is still open and how long it will take.
